## Setup

The corpus has 204 documents (72 wikipedia, 120 arxiv, 12 mirror) across 6 topics (compilers, computer_vision, cryptography, database_internals, distributed_systems, nlp), indexed as **359 chunks**. Dense embeddings come from `voyage-4`, and the sparse side is BM25 with server-side IDF, both stored in Qdrant. For routing, I fit 8 clusters with spherical k-means, choosing k by cosine silhouette. **81.3%** of chunks land in a cluster whose majority topic is their own, even though the clusterer never sees topic labels.

The 290 queries (140 wikipedia, 120 arxiv, 30 multihop) were written by Claude and are frozen in `data/eval/queries.jsonl`. Each single-hop query (`wikipedia`, `arxiv`) comes from one sampled passage, and that passage's document is the relevant one. Multi-hop queries (`multihop`) come from passages of two same-topic articles and need both, so both documents count as relevant.

To add redundancy on purpose, the corpus includes near-duplicate `mirror` copies of some articles: the lead and headings are kept, and ~20% of the other paragraphs are dropped. A mirror is scored as its original, so it can't earn extra credit or add to the distinct-document count, but its chunks can still crowd other documents out of the top k.

All metrics use document-level relevance at cutoff k=10, where a document counts at the rank of its first chunk. For multi-hop queries, Recall@10 is the fraction of the two relevant documents retrieved.

### Configurations

- **Dense only**: Voyage embeddings, cosine kNN
- **BM25 only**: Sparse BM25, server-side IDF
- **Hybrid (RRF)**: Dense + BM25, reciprocal rank fusion
- **Hybrid + routing (m=1)**: Dense branch restricted to nearest cluster
- **Hybrid + routing (m=3)**: Dense branch restricted to 3 nearest clusters
- **Hybrid + MMR (λ=0.7) (default)**: MMR over the fused candidate pool: the shipped configuration
- **Hybrid + routing + MMR (m=3, λ=0.7)**: Routing and MMR combined

## Caveats

Only the source document(s) of each query are judged relevant. Other documents that also answer it count as misses, which makes the absolute scores a lower bound; the comparisons between rows are the meaningful signal.

The queries are written from specific passages. Claude was told to paraphrase, but the queries can still share vocabulary with their passages, which may favor lexical (BM25) matching compared with real user queries.

The mirrors are mechanical copies, more uniform than real-world overlap between documents. They isolate the redundancy effect rather than model it faithfully.

MMR optimizes for diversity, not relevance, but it can still move nDCG in either direction. Because a document counts at the rank of its first chunk, demoting redundant chunks of other documents can lift the relevant one, while demoting a chunk that's too similar to one already chosen can push it down. The diversity table shows the intended effect, and the Δ column shows the net effect on ranking quality.

The confidence intervals come from 2,000 bootstrap resamples over queries (seed 0).
